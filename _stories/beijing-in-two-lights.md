Beijing revealed itself first from a distance. At the airport, the city lay somewhere beyond the runways, while aircraft crossed a thinning band of gold. Two evenings later, I followed the fading light from the old city into Olympic Park and onward, until roads and towers had taken possession of the dark.

{% include photo-strip.html id="approach" set=page.photo_sets.approach %}

At Capital Airport, Beijing feels both near and unreachable. The terminal sits low beneath the mountains; above it, each arriving aircraft carries the sunset for a few seconds longer, then descends and gives it back to the horizon.

{% include photo-strip.html id="old-city" set=page.photo_sets.old_city %}

In the old city, the vastness folds inward. The eye settles on moat water, painted eaves and the papery brightness of ginkgo leaves. Then, from higher ground, the view opens again: palace roofs stream toward a skyline that belongs to another century.

{% include photo-strip.html id="olympic-park" set=page.photo_sets.olympic_park %}

By the time I reached Olympic Park, the last yellow leaves were still holding daylight in their branches. It did not last. One by one, the tower, the stadium and the temporary installations began to glow, and the park exchanged autumn gold for neon.

{% include photo-strip.html id="city-lines" set=page.photo_sets.city_lines %}

Farther into the city, I slowed the shutter and let the traffic write its own restless lines. Roads that seemed merely to divide the buildings now bound them together, drawing the scattered lights of Beijing into one continuous night.
